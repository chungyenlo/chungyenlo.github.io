## Job Market Paper

**Concentration without Greater Market Power? Evidence from a Sugar Refining Acquisition in the United States**  
[Latest version (PDF)](/files/Lo_JMP.pdf){:target="_blank"}

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract</b></summary>
This paper examines whether a merger between sugar refiners affects competition in the retail markets where their brands compete. Using NielsenIQ Retail Scanner data from 2016 to 2022, I study a 2021 acquisition between two U.S. sugar refiners. A difference-in-differences design comparing the Southeast, where the merging firms' refining capacity is concentrated, with other regions finds no relative increase in retail prices after the announcement. I then estimate a random-coefficients logit demand model and recover markups and marginal costs under multiproduct Bertrand competition. A counterfactual decomposition separates ownership and cost channels within a common demand environment. The ownership change raises equilibrium prices by only 0.03 percent and leaves markups, profits, and welfare essentially unchanged, while cost differences between the two periods lower prices by 0.4 percent and markups by 2.1 percent. The merging firms account for under 4 percent of retail volume, and substitution is spread across many brands, with private label dominating the category. The results suggest that a producer merger's competitive effects depend less on the change in producer concentration than on how much of the consumer-facing market the merging firms control and how readily buyers substitute between their products.
